How to play

Shooting

Sling it. Press on cleared ground (never fog or a solid pixel), then drag away from where you want the ball to fly — like pulling back a slingshot. The ball spawns where you pressed and launches in the opposite direction on release. The drag only sets the direction: every ball always leaves at its own fixed speed, improved by its speed upgrades. You can still fire into the dark from cleared ground.

Primary balls never stop. Every round ricochets off pixels and steel forever. They start modestly: damage growth, crushes, pierce, chains and speed growth must be earned. Small fragments expire after 18 seconds and cannot reproduce.

Rime, Cairn and Volt are available from the start. Rime prepares frost, Cairn specializes in earth impacts, and Volt grows into charge or velocity. Each has 22 upgrades, including two mutually exclusive specializations. Glacier or Hail; Tectonic or Obelisk; Storm or Rail. Choosing one seals the entire rival branch. Field adds 15 shared upgrades. Select a node to inspect it, then use its purchase button. Key upgrades change the ball's silhouette, colour and dock icon, including balls already in flight.

Build a team. Rime's cyan frost lasts a few seconds; Cairn's Earth Strike instantly shatters it, even on tough pixels. Amber fault cracks amplify later direct hits from any ball. Volt's violet charge can feed lightning chains or Cairn's Grounding burst. Ice Conductor chains immediately through frozen targets. Stack all three coatings for Field's Convergence bonus. Pulses and fragments have their own supporting upgrades; fragments never inherit a parent's entire tree.

Right-drag or WASD pans the camera. Wheel zooms at the cursor. Click the field map to jump.

The field

One million pixel cells, each five ball-diameters wide. Every undamaged pixel is the same grey; as it loses health it heats through yellow → orange → red. Steel is white. Pixels near the starting arena are still the weakest; health rises with distance from the centre, up to 40 at the outer edges.

Every cell is a pixel except the small starting arena. Most are breakable; the steel ones are not.

The dark hides the field. A pixel only shows once a neighbouring pixel is broken — each impact reveals 3 pixels deep. Fog is pitch black; ground you have already cleared is a shade lighter. Your shots still collide with the dark, so every blind shot lights up its surroundings.

Steel pixels are unbreakable and form one connected web: dashed arteries across the whole field, decorated with rings, wheels, ladders, combs, zigzags, polygons and gates. They never enclose a region — every breakable pixel stays reachable, on every seed. They block and bounce shots, so dig around them. Cairn can learn capped steel growth; Volt can turn steel ricochets into speed. Frost and shatter never destroy steel.

Economy

Destroying pixels earns ◆, which pays for launching balls. Every ball has its own launch price and reload time. Each launch adds a demand surcharge (+20% of the base price, up to 2.6×), but it cools while that ball rests — spamming one ball briefly gets expensive, yet the price always settles back down. Switching balls or pacing your shots keeps launches cheap.

Glowing shards are always visible, even in the dark. Break one to earn ✦ skill points and unlock tech. Far shards are worth more.

Damage colours

Pixels start grey; the brighter and warmer they glow, the closer they are to breaking. Each point of damage removes one health; tougher outer pixels take many more hits.
