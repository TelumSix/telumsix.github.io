How to play

Shooting

Sling it. Press on cleared ground (never fog or a solid pixel), then drag away from where you want the ball to fly — like pulling back a slingshot. The ball spawns where you pressed and launches in the opposite direction on release. The drag only sets the direction: every ball always leaves at its own fixed speed, improved by its speed upgrades. You can still fire into the dark from cleared ground.

Primary balls never stop. Every round ricochets off pixels and steel forever. They start modestly: damage growth, crushes, pierce, chains and speed growth must be earned. Small fragments expire after 18 seconds and cannot reproduce.

Rime, Cairn and Volt are available from the start. Each has 34 upgrades; Field adds 24 more. Choose Glacier or Hail, Tectonic or Obelisk, Storm or Rail. More exclusive side choices shape your build, while shared talents work with either specialization. Late gateways reopen selected rival talents; the rival specialization stays sealed. Teal connections show these return routes. Click a node to buy it, drag to pan, or use the zoom controls. Balls always stay round: upgrades change their surface, rim, colour and trails, including balls already in flight.

Build a team. Rime's cyan frost lasts a few seconds; Cairn's Earth Strike instantly shatters it, even on tough pixels. Amber fault cracks amplify later direct hits from any ball. Volt's violet charge can feed lightning chains or Cairn's Grounding burst. Ice Conductor chains immediately through frozen targets. Stack all three coatings for Field's Convergence bonus. Pulses and fragments have their own supporting upgrades; fragments never inherit a parent's entire tree.

Pierce means pass-through. Each charge lets a ball deal its normal hit damage and travel through one surviving pixel, reaching targets behind it. It does not add damage or pass through steel. The final charge carries it through the whole pixel; hitting that pixel again later deals damage normally. A pale arc around the ball shows its remaining charges. Focus adds starting charges; Ice Needle and Rail recharge them through bounces. Pierce is useful for spreading coatings, reaching deeper shards, and feeding Phase Drive.

Right-drag or WASD pans the camera. Wheel zooms at the cursor. Click the field map to jump.

The field

One million pixel cells, each five ball-diameters wide. Untouched pixels are grey stone or brown soil in scattered patches. Their first damaging hit reveals their remaining health colour: high HP is violet/blue, then green, yellow, orange and red as it falls. A colour always means the same remaining HP. Pixel health has doubled, reaching 80 HP; shards now have 2 HP. Steel remains white and unbreakable.

Every cell is a pixel except the small starting arena. Most are breakable; the steel ones are not.

The dark hides the field. A pixel only shows once a neighbouring pixel is broken — each impact reveals 3 pixels deep. Fog is pitch black; ground you have already cleared is a shade lighter. Your shots still collide with the dark, so every blind shot lights up its surroundings.

Steel pixels are unbreakable and form one connected web: dashed arteries across the whole field, decorated with rings, wheels, ladders, combs, zigzags, polygons and gates. They never enclose a region — every breakable pixel stays reachable, on every seed. They block and bounce shots, so dig around them. Cairn can learn capped steel growth; Volt can turn steel ricochets into speed. Frost and shatter never destroy steel.

Economy

Destroying pixels earns ◆, which pays for launching balls. A new game starts with 150 currency; base prices are Rime 24, Cairn 48 and Volt 32. Pixel payouts grow gently with toughness: about 5 currency for an 8-HP pixel and 11 for an 80-HP pixel before upgrades.

Building a fleet raises prices permanently. Every primary ball launched increases its own type's price and contributes to shared fleet growth. Switching types helps, but cannot erase that investment cost. A separate 30% demand surcharge per recent shot cools at 0.35 units per second, capped at 2.8× the investment price. Twin Launch costs 60% extra per shot and records both primary balls. Economy upgrades remain useful; upgrading balls already in flight is an efficient way to grow stronger.

Your investment persists. Primary balls, their earned growth and launch counts survive saves. Temporary sparks and pixel coatings expire on reload. Older saves keep their currency; a launch reserve prevents returning players from being stranded without a fleet.

Glowing shards are always visible, even in the dark. Break one to earn ✦ skill points and unlock tech. Far shards are worth more.

Damage colours

Grey stone and brown soil conceal their health until hit. After that, colour shows remaining HP rather than damage percentage. Frost, fault cracks and charge appear as extra coatings.
